Dating the law codes: where the scholars agree and differ

Each law code gets one bar per scholar who dates it: Liebermann (1903), Attenborough (1922), Robertson (1925), Whitelock (EHD), Wormald (1999) and others. A thin bar is the whole range given; a thick stretch is the date they think likely (“probably”, “perhaps”); a fading bar is open-ended (“before 918”, “after 893”). Hover for the scholar's own words. Sort by disagreement to bring the contested codes, such as EGu, I–II Cnut and the Edgar codes, to the top.

An experiment, made from the dates in oe-laws/codes.json, which come from <creation><date> in the clause files. Years are read from free text, so treat them as approximate; dates given only relative to another code (“after IV Eg”) appear in the tooltip but not as bars.
