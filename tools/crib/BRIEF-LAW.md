# Addendum to BRIEF.md: the legal texts

Follow `BRIEF.md` (input, output, semi-literal method, square brackets, "[?]", plain text with `<i>` only, report the real language), with these additions for the legal texts: the Leges Henrici Primi (Hn), Leges Edwardi Confessoris (ECf) and its *retractatus* (ECf retr), Quadripartitus (Quadr), the Latin paraphrase of VI Æthelred, the Consiliatio Cnuti (Cons Cn), Pseudo-Cnut *de foresta*, Libertas Londoniensis, William I's Ten Articles (Latin *retractatus* and Anglo-Norman French), and William I's policy in Eadmer.

## Terms
Use the established English of Anglo-Norman legal history (as in Downer's Hn, O'Brien's ECf, Stubbs and Robertson), consistently:
- *wera, were, weregildum* → "wergild"; *wita, wite* → "wite"; *manbota* → "manbot"; *bot, emendatio* → "compensation" (or "amends" where the sense is moral); *emendare* → "to pay compensation (for)", "to make amends"; *forisfacere* → "to commit an offence", "to forfeit" (by context); *forisfactura* → "forfeiture" or "fine" (by context); *forisfactum* → "offence".
- *placitum* → "plea" (a case) or "court"; *placitare* → "to plead"; *causa* → "case"; *iudicium* → "judgment"; *iudices* → "judges", *iudicatores* → "doomsmen"; *iustitia* → "justice" (the official, where it is a person: "the justice"), *iustitiarius* → "justiciar"; *socna / soca*, *saca et soca* → "soke", "sake and soke"; *tol et team*, *infangenetheof* → keep as "toll and team", "infangentheof".
- *comitatus* → "shire" (the county and its court); *hundredum*, *wapentagium* → "hundred", "wapentake"; *decimatio, decima, friborgus, francplegium* → "tithing", "frankpledge"; *burgus* → "borough"; *portus* → "port"; *halimotum* → "hallmoot".
- *vicecomes* → "sheriff"; *praepositus* → "reeve"; *comes* → "earl"; *baro* → "baron"; *thainus, tainus* → "thegn"; *liber homo* → "free man"; *villanus* → "villein"; *cotsetus* → "cottar"; *servus* → "slave" (Anglo-Saxon context) or "serf" (where the text is clearly about Norman villeinage); *dominus* (of a man) → "lord"; *homo* (of a lord) → "man", "vassal" only where the text says so.
- *pax regis* → "the king's peace"; *grithbrech*, *hamsocna*, *forsteal*, *fihtwite*, *murdrum*, *ordel*, *lagu* and other Old English legal words quoted in the Latin: keep them, in italics, with a short English gloss in round brackets the first time in your batch: "<i>hamsocn</i> (attack on a house)".
- *iusiurandum, sacramentum* → "oath"; *iurare* → "swear"; *purgare se* → "clear himself"; *lada* → "exculpation"; *cum XII* → "with twelve [oath-helpers]"; *iuramentum plenum, planum* → "full oath, plain oath".
- *Christianitas* → "Christianity" (= church law and the Church's jurisdiction) or "the Church"; *episcopus, archiepiscopus, presbyter, clericus, ordinatus* → "bishop, archbishop, priest, cleric, man in orders".
- Money: *solidus* → "shilling"; *denarius* → "penny"; *libra* → "pound"; *mancusa* → "mancus"; *marca* → "mark"; *ora* → "ora".
- Laws and texts referred to by name (*lex Westsexe, Merchenelahe, Denelahe*; *lex Salica*): "the law of Wessex", "the Mercian law", "the Danelaw".

## Style
- These are statutes and a treatise, not liturgy: keep the legal structure clear ("If anyone …, let him pay …"); render the subjunctive of command as "let him …" or "he shall …", consistently within a text.
- Keep cross-references to clause numbers as they stand ("as is said above, 12, 3"), translating only the words; keep Liebermann's notes inside the text (sigla, "o. S.", "ebd.") translated as in BRIEF.md ("above p.", "ibid.").
- Quadripartitus is itself a Latin translation of Old English laws: translate *its Latin*, not the Old English behind it.
- The ECf retractatus repeats much of ECf: where the two texts say the same thing, use the same English, so that the differences stand out.
- Anglo-Norman French (Wl art Frz): translate as literally as good English allows; report "xno".
