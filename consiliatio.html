Consiliatio Cnuti, read in order

The Consiliatio Cnuti, a Latin translation of Cnut’s laws made in the reign of Henry I (Liebermann: c. 1110–30), read from beginning to end.

About this text and this page

Liebermann never prints the Consiliatio as one continuous text. It translates I and II Cnut, adds a little from VIII Æthelred, and ends with three short texts, Be blaserum, Forfang and the Hundred Ordinance. He prints each passage once only, as a Latin column beside the Old English it translates. His list of the work’s chapters (Gesetze I, pp. 618–619) gives in full only what he has not printed beside a source (the inscription, the author’s prooemium, a passage on tithings at II 19,2a–d, one on the fore-oath at II 22,1b, and a few closing sentences), and points with a short quotation (“De lege seculari … — — perquirendi; s. o. S. 309—323”) to the rest. This page puts the passages back together in the translator’s order, following that list. Each passage is labelled with its chapter of the Consiliatio and links to the clause page where Liebermann prints it, with his notes and the Old English beside it.

Numbering. The three books are Liebermann’s division. Books I and II translate I and II Cnut and are numbered to match them, so that Cons Cn II 30 translates II Cnut 30; the translator’s own additions carry letters (II 19,2a–d). Book III is the three short texts (Blas, Forf, Hu). The title is Liebermann’s: he made it from the translator’s word for the three prologues (consiliatio).

The author and the manuscripts. Liebermann dates the work after 1102 and probably before c. 1130: the author renders English words in their Norman-period sense, writes in a more scholastic and canonistic way than the Instituta Cnuti, Quadripartitus or the Leges Henrici, and credits Cnut alone with uniting the law; he seems to have known Kent. Only Hr (London, BL Harley 1704, c. 1325) is complete, and there the Leges Edwardi Confessoris follow as if part of the same work. Cb (Paris, BnF lat. 4771, c. 1160) has only the prologue, I Cn 1 f. and the end (Hu 8 f.), grafted into its Instituta Cnuti; Hk (c. 1230) has about a third of the work, in twenty pieces. (Liebermann, Gesetze I, p. 618, note a, and his Consiliatio Cnuti, Halle 1893.)

The English. Beside the Latin is an English crib. For the passages printed beside Cnut, VIII Æthelred, Blas, Forf and Hu, it is the crib of the Old English source (made from Liebermann’s German translation), so that the Latin can be compared with what it translates; where the translator departs from his source, the two will differ. For the passages found only in Liebermann’s list, it is a crib of the Latin itself. Both were made with the help of an LLM (Claude, 2026) and have not been checked line by line. A label under each passage says which it is.
