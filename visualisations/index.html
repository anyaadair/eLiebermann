Visualisation Tools

Interactive pictures of the laws and the dictionary, drawn from the same data as the rest of eLiebermann. Each tool sets out one kind of connection: between law codes, across time and place, or among words and subjects. Click and filter to explore; each tool says how it was made.

Borrowing and reuse

How the law codes draw on one another, from the clause-to-clause comparisons of Liebermann and later scholars.

Which law codes borrow from which

Arcs or network

Arcs between law codes, from the clause-to-clause comparisons of Liebermann, Wormald, Kennedy, Whitelock and Jurasinski and Oliver. Click a code or an arc to see the clauses behind it.

Chains of reuse from one clause

Tree

Follow a clause forward to the later clauses that take it up, and back to the ones it draws on.

Time, place and manuscripts

When and where the codes were made, and the manuscripts that carry them.

Dating the law codes

Timeline

Each scholar’s date for each code, side by side, to show where they agree and where they differ.

Where the law codes were made

Map

The places where the codes were issued, with a slider through time.

Which manuscripts carry which law codes

Timeline network

Law codes at the date they were made, joined to the manuscripts and early prints that carry them at the date they were written.

Words and subjects

The vocabulary of the laws, from Liebermann’s Wörterbuch, and the subjects tagged on the clauses.

Law codes by shared vocabulary

Network

Law codes joined by the Wörterbuch headwords that cite both, with a headword search.

Liebermann’s cross-references between headwords

Network

Word families from Liebermann’s s., vgl. and Der., one to three steps out from a headword.

Subjects that appear together

Network

Subject keywords joined when they are tagged on the same clauses more often than chance.

The tools are made from the clause and entry files by visualisations/build.py (see the README), and are rebuilt when the data changes. They work best on a large screen.
